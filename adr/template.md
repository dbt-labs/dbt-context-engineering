# NNNN. Short title of the decision

- Status: Proposed
- Date: YYYY-MM-DD

## Concept

The mental model behind the decision, from first principles. State the idea, and where one helps,
the analogy that makes it intuitive. Bold the key terms a reader must hold. Use this section to
explain why the idea exists at all, before the constraints.

## Context

The forces at play: the problem, the constraints, and what makes the decision non-obvious. Where the
decision came down to a choice, enumerate the alternatives considered as a numbered list, each with
a short verdict. State facts, not the decision.

## Decision

The decision, in active voice ("We will ..."). Lead with a single bolded thesis a reader can hold in
one line. Be specific enough that someone can tell whether the code conforms: name the macro, the
construct, the column, the default. Include a code block showing the shape where it clarifies.

## Reasoning

Walk the logic from first principles. A short run of paragraphs, each led by a bold "Why ..."
question and answered from the constraints in Context, so a reader sees not just what was decided but
why each alternative fell away.

## Consequences

What becomes easier and what becomes harder, as a bullet list, with the key phrase bolded in each.
Include costs and follow-on work, and link related ADRs by number.

## Glossary

Define every domain term a non-expert reader needs, one bolded term per entry, so the record stays
readable by someone who does not already know the codebase.
